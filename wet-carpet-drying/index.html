Wet Carpet Drying: What West New York Homeowners Should Know

There is always more water in a soaked carpet than the surface suggests. Up top the fibers may seem barely damp, yet the pad has filled up the way a sponge does and the subfloor under it has gotten wet as well. Towels and a shop vacuum reach the top layer and little else. For West New York homes, our crew handles the extraction, meters the carpet, pad and subfloor, and runs drying gear that gets to the bottom layer.

Two things decide if a carpet is worth saving: the cleanliness of the water and the time it has sat wet. Carpet soaked by a clean supply line can usually be dried in place if we get to it quickly. Gray water from an appliance often means the pad goes and the carpet gets cleaned and treated. After sewage or flood water from outdoors, neither the carpet nor the pad stays.

What It Looks Like

  • Carpet that squishes underfoot or leaves your socks damp when you walk on it
  • Yellow or brown discoloration rising to the fiber tips while the carpet dries
  • Days after the water was cleaned up, the carpet is cool and clammy to the touch
  • Lift a corner and the backing peels away from the carpet
  • A wet spot that keeps growing even though no new water has been added
  • Soft or squeaky subfloor beneath the area of carpet that was soaked
What We Do

How We Handle Wet Carpet Drying

1

Map the Wet Area

Our first job is finding the outer limit of the water, under walls and in closets too, using a thermal camera and moisture meters. The water category gets identified as well, since it determines what we can keep.

2

Extract the Water

With weighted extractors, carpet and the pad beneath it are pressed and emptied in one pass. A wand by itself leaves much more behind, so this step cuts the drying time afterward.

3

Dry All the Layers

Some jobs call for floating the carpet on a cushion of air, others for lifting it and taking the pad out. Air movers and dehumidifiers run until carpet and subfloor both read dry.

4

Clean and Reset

Where needed, an antimicrobial goes on the subfloor and carpet, then new pad is laid if the old was removed, and the carpet is stretched back on its tack strip and cleaned.

What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

Furniture Marks

When carpet is wet, the finish on wooden legs and the rust from metal glides transfer into it. The furniture also draws up water and swells, and the marks it leaves often never come out.

Clean Water Turns Dirty

Water that started clean does not stay that way. Soil and bacteria build up in it inside the carpet and pad, and a carpet that was dryable can end up being torn out.

Mold Under the Carpet

Mold tends to start underneath, on the carpet back, because that side stays damp and dark. You smell it long before you ever see it on top.

Soaked Pad Underneath

A carpet pad absorbs many times what it weighs in water. Long after the top feels dry, that pad is still passing moisture up into the carpet and down into the floor.

The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

The first few hours

Water Spreads Sideways

Water travels through the pad far beyond the patch you can see. It slips beneath walls into neighboring rooms while the tack strip begins to absorb it.

Hours 24 to 48

Odor and Mold Begin

Bacteria in the damp pad start producing that sour wet carpet smell. Mold can begin growing on the backing and on dust trapped deep in the fibers.

Day 3 to 7

Carpet Starts Failing

As the latex bonding the backing gives out, the carpet delaminates and starts to ripple. Rust forms on the tack strip nails while the subfloor underneath swells.

Past one week

Too Late to Save

By now the carpet and pad usually have to come out. Often mold has spread to the subfloor and the base of the drywall, which adds to the job.
