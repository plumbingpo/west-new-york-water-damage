Hardwood Floor Water Damage: What West New York Homeowners Should Know

It does not take long for water to change a wood floor. As the wood absorbs water along its edges and bottom face, it expands, crowds the neighboring boards and cups, leaving the center lower than the sides. A floor in this condition is not necessarily ruined. Solid hardwood that is dried in the first few days often flattens back out. What matters is removing moisture from the wood and from its subfloor steadily and under control.

Our tool for hardwood is the floor drying mat. A vacuum unit attached to the sealed mats lifts water out by way of the seams and the wood itself, and dehumidifiers hold the room air at a dry level. Compared with carpet or drywall it is slower, commonly a week or more. Sanding has to wait until the wood is fully dry. A cupped floor sanded early ends up crowned afterward.

Signs to Look For

  • Fasteners working loose, heard as squeaks where the floor was soaked
  • Cupping, where each board's edges rise above its middle
  • Mildew smell coming up through the boards
  • Puffy baseboards and casings next to the affected boards
  • Crowning, meaning the middle of each board bulges above its edges
  • A hazy, whitened or flaking finish where the water sat
If Nothing Is Done

How the Damage Progresses

The first few hours

Water Goes Under

Seams let the water through, and it fans out in the layer between flooring and subfloor. You can towel off the top and still have a saturated underside.

24 to 48 hours

Cupping Starts

Boards swell and the edges rise. A cloudy look can appear in the finish. Growth is able to begin below, on raw wood and on the subfloor panels.

Days 3 through 7

Pressure Builds Up

Pressure grows through the whole floor as the wood expands. Edges get crushed together, nails work loose and parts of the floor may start rising from the subfloor.

Past one week

Permanent Damage

Buckling, black staining and mold under the boards become likely. By now replacement, not drying, is the answer for most of the affected section.

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Wet Subfloor

Long after the boards seem dry, the subfloor may still be holding water. Leave it wet and the cupping returns or mold grows up from below.

Cracks From Rushed Drying

High heat or too much airflow pulls moisture from the surface faster than the core can follow. The result can be cracks, splits and permanent gaps between boards.

Damage Beyond the Room

Once below the boards, water moves. Next door rooms, the area under cabinets and walls, and the ceiling below are all places it can end up.

Mold Under Boards

No air moves and no light reaches the gap between the hardwood and the subfloor. You will smell it before you see it, because nothing shows on the surface.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Measure the Moisture

Readings are taken in the boards and the subfloor, and on dry flooring close by as a reference. The comparison shows how far down moisture reached and how likely the floor is to recover.

2

Extract Standing Water

Rugs and furnishings come off the affected boards while pooled water is vacuumed up at once. Boards swell less when the seams stop taking in water sooner.

3

Set Drying Mats

A vacuum system hooked to mats sealed on the surface lifts moisture out of the boards from above. The room is managed with dehumidifiers so drying stays even and the boards do not crack.

4

Track Progress and Report

Readings are logged every one to two days and the equipment stays until the wet zone equals the dry comparison areas. Then we tell you whether the floor needs refinishing, a few boards replaced, or nothing.
