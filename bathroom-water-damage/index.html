Bathroom Water Damage in West New York

Tile, tubs and fixtures are made to get wet on top, but what lies beneath them is not. When caulk cracks, grout wears thin or a toilet seal fails, a little water slips past with every shower and every flush. That water gathers where you cannot see it, in the subfloor and inside the tiled wall. In West New York, we locate the entry point, measure how far the water has spread, then dry or take out the wet material.

These leaks are usually slow, so the damage is often deeper and older than it appears. Under a toilet, the subfloor may be soft from top to bottom even though nothing looks wrong with the tile. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map the wet area without tearing out more than we need to, then open only the sections that cannot be dried in place.

What It Looks Like

  • Directly under the bathroom, a ceiling with a brown stain or a sag
  • A toilet that rocks slightly, or dark staining around its base
  • Grout lines that stay dark and damp long after the last shower
  • Baseboard puffing up or paint coming off near the shower wall
  • Edges of the vinyl floor peeling up, or dark discoloration showing through it
  • Inside the vanity, a base shelf that is stained, soft or out of shape

Why Speed Matters

How the Damage Progresses

First days

Water Gets Past

Each shower pushes a little water through the failed caulk or grout. Backer board and the outer rim of the subfloor take it in, and there is still nothing to see.

First few weeks

The Subfloor Never Dries

The wood under the tile never gets a chance to dry between showers. Mold gets going in the wall cavity and on the floor's underside.

One to three months

Signs Start to Appear

Grout darkens, tiles loosen, and the caulk line turns black. Where a room sits underneath, its ceiling develops a stain that gradually spreads.

Six months and beyond

The Structure Suffers

Around the tub or toilet the subfloor goes soft, and the joists may start rotting. The repair now includes carpentry and new tile, not only drying.

What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Leaks Inside the Vanity

Behind the towels and bottles kept under the sink, a drain fitting or supply valve can drip for months. Water swells the cabinet bottom, then passes into the back wall and the subfloor.

Mold Behind Tile

Neither grout nor tile is a waterproof barrier without help. If the board behind them stays damp, mold grows on it, out of reach of any cleaning.

Poor Ventilation

Without a working exhaust fan, the steam from showers has nowhere to go. That moisture settles on walls and ceilings, feeds mold, and slows the drying of anything that is already wet.

Damage Downstairs

Before dripping, water runs along joists and pipes, which can put the downstairs stain several feet away from the real leak. Light fixtures and wiring can be in its path.

What We Do

How We Handle Bathroom Water Damage

1

Find the Leak

We test the tub, shower, toilet and supply lines one at a time, and use moisture meters and thermal imaging to see where water has traveled under tile and inside walls.

2

Open What Is Needed

Loose tile, rotted subfloor and soft wall board come out. A toilet leak means contamination, so anything porous that will not come clean is removed.

3

Dry the Cavities

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the subfloor, the wall framing and the ceiling space below. Before anything gets closed up, antimicrobial is applied and dry readings are confirmed.

4

Rebuild and Seal

New subfloor and backer board go in, and we can coordinate the tile, toilet seal, caulk and ceiling work, meaning the path of the leak gets closed and not just hidden.
