Attic Water Damage: What West New York Homeowners Should Know

Attic water damage comes from two directions. Rain gets in from above through worn shingles, failed flashing, or the seals around vents and chimneys. The house itself sends moisture upward too, and in a poorly ventilated attic it condenses on cold sheathing. Whichever it is, few people ever go up and look. Our team in West New York inspects the attic, finds the source, and dries it out.

On the typical attic job, soaked insulation is the main issue. Once wet, both cellulose and fiberglass lose R-value, and heating and cooling the home costs more. Even worse, it keeps water pressed against the framing and the ceiling drywall for weeks. We remove what is saturated, dry the wood with air movers and dehumidifiers, and treat any mold on the rafters or sheathing.

Warning Signs

  • Attic ducts and metal hangers showing drips, rust or water marks
  • A patch of insulation that is flatter, more matted, or darker than the rest
  • Daylight showing around a vent pipe, chimney, or along a roof valley
  • Musty odor in upstairs closets or in the attic itself
  • Black or white fuzzy growth on the sheathing, often heaviest near the eaves
  • Rafters and roof decking marked with dark streaks or stains
If Nothing Is Done

The Damage Timeline

The first storm

Insulation Soaks It Up

The insulation catches and absorbs whatever rain gets by the roofing. Because the rooms below show nothing, the leak is missed and no roof repair happens.

24 to 48 hours

Mold Can Start

Damp wood and paper facing in a warm, still attic are ideal for mold. Growth can begin on the sheathing and on the back of the ceiling drywall.

A few weeks on

Stains Come Through

Repeated wetting saturates the insulation, and water gets down to the ceiling. In the room underneath, brown rings show up and the drywall may begin sagging.

Months later

Rot Sets In

The sheathing comes apart in layers and no longer grips the roofing nails. Mold spreads across large sections of the roof deck, and the rafters remain damp enough to decay.

Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Mold on Sheathing

Poorly ventilated attics often grow mold across the underside of the sheathing. A wide area may be covered by the time somebody climbs up to check.

Air Conditioner Drips

In a lot of homes the attic houses an air handler along with its condensate line and pan. When the pan overflows or the drain clogs, insulation gets wet for weeks without any rain as a warning.

Insulation That Stops Working

Insulation that is damp and compressed barely insulates, and drying may not bring it back. The rooms beneath that section get colder or hotter, and you pay more for energy.

Water Near Electrical

Attics hold junction boxes, recessed light housings and cable runs. When they get wet there is a risk of shock and fire, so keep out until the power has been checked.

How We Fix It

Our Attic Water Damage Process

1

Inspect and Find the Source

To learn whether you have a leak, condensation or the two together, we go over the roof deck, the vents, the exhaust ducts and each flashing point. Its full reach is mapped with a thermal camera and meter readings.

2

Remove Wet Insulation

Insulation that is soaked or has mold gets bagged and carried out, giving the wood and the drywall below a chance to dry. The access path is kept contained and the living area underneath is protected.

3

Dry the Framing

With dehumidifiers and air movers, the sheathing, joists and rafters return to normal moisture levels. We clean visible mold off the wood, then apply an antimicrobial.

4

Correct the Source

You are shown the ventilation or roof problem to be corrected, and our findings can go to your roofer. New insulation is installed to bring back the R-value after the attic is staying dry.
