Water Heater Leak Cleanup in West New York: What to Know

The tank on a water heater stores 40 to 80 gallons, yet a leak can release far more than that. Your supply line keeps feeding the tank, which means it fills back up just as quickly as water escapes through a rusted bottom. It will run until the cold water valve gets closed. Where the unit sits decides the damage: a garage floor, a hallway closet or, worst of all, an attic above finished rooms.

After extracting the water, our West New York crew checks the materials that sat next to the unit. Usually the tank platform and the lower part of the closet walls have taken on the most water. In a garage, water reaches the wall shared with the house and anything stored on the floor. Attic leaks travel downward through the insulation and the ceilings below. We measure each area and dry it with air movers and dehumidifiers.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Cut the Power or GasFor an electric heater, switch off its breaker. For gas, turn the control knob to off. Running the burner or the element with no water in the tank is something to avoid every time.
  2. Shut the Cold Water ValveThe cold line over the tank has a valve, and it closes with a clockwise turn. If it is stuck or leaking, shut off the main water valve for the house.
  3. Beware of Scalding WaterWhat comes out of the tank can cause scalds. Pets and children should stay clear, and nobody should walk into pooled water before it cools down.
  4. Empty the Tank OutsideWhen the drain valve is within reach, connect a garden hose and lead it to a floor drain or outdoors, which keeps the rest of the water off the floor.
  5. Clear the FloorAnything absorbent, including boxes and rugs, should come up from the wet area. Cardboard and fabric soak up water quickly and hold it against walls and concrete.
Coverage

Insurance and Water Heater Leak Cleanup

When a tank fails suddenly, the water damage to floors, walls and belongings is often covered as sudden and accidental. Replacing the heater is usually not included, because a tank wearing out from age counts as maintenance. Rust trails, old staining or other evidence that the leak was long-term can lead an adjuster to reduce or deny the claim, since gradual leaks usually fall outside coverage. Until the claim is closed, hold on to the old heater. Photograph it along with the damage, and review your policy.

Common Causes

Why This Happens

  • A worn anode rod that no longer protects the tank lining from rust.
  • An overflow pan that cannot drain, because its line is clogged or the pan itself has rusted.
  • Excess pressure in the tank from a failed thermostat or a missing expansion tank.
  • A temperature and pressure relief valve that opens or fails and discharges water.
  • Loose plumbing connections left after a recent installation or repair.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

Adjacent Room Flooring

From a heater closet, the leak slips beneath the door and reaches hallway hardwood, laminate or carpet. Heat makes it worse, with quicker swelling of wood boards and carpet backing that comes apart.

Items Stored Near the Heater

Boxes, furniture and keepsakes stored near a garage or closet heater sit in the water first. Cardboard falls apart, and wet contents can mold if they are not dried or discarded quickly.

Ceilings Under an Attic Heater

Water from an attic unit runs down through insulation to the ceiling beneath it. Wet insulation stops insulating and its weight can bring ceiling drywall down.

Stand and Subfloor Damage

More water goes into the subfloor or wooden stand beneath the tank than anywhere else. It can rot and weaken, which matters when a new full tank is set on it.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Extract the Water

Our crew extracts the water standing in the closet, the garage or on the floor, plus the overflow pan and the cavity above the ceiling on attic jobs, so that materials around it quit absorbing it.

2

Check Around the Heater

To learn the full reach of the water, our crew meters and scans the platform, the bottoms of the walls, neighboring rooms and the ceiling below if there is one.

3

Take Out What Will Not Dry

Soaked insulation, crumbling drywall and damaged baseboards are taken out. For an attic leak, the wet insulation is cleared out, which lets us dry the drywall and ceiling joists beneath.

4

Drying and Moisture Readings

Drying usually takes 3 to 5 days, and dehumidifiers and air movers run the whole time. We record moisture readings and tell you when the space is ready for the new heater.
