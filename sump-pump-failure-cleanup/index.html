Sump Pump Failure Cleanup in West New York: What to Know

Sump pumps do a single task, yet they commonly quit right when that task matters. Heavy rain raises the water table, the pit fills, and then the power goes out, the float sticks or the pump cannot keep up. From there it tops the edge of the basin and runs out over the slab. Until pumping resumes, the water continues to rise.

This water is not clean. It has come through soil and often carries silt, bacteria and whatever was in the drain tile, so we treat it as Category 2 at best and Category 3 if it has sat or mixed with sewage. For the job, this means soaked porous items such as drywall and carpet pad are usually discarded, and what stays gets washed and disinfected first, then dried.

First Five Minutes

Your First Steps

  1. Keep Out of the BasementDo not step into a flooded basement while the power is on. Outlets, the pump cord or the furnace might already be wet and live.
  2. Turn Off the Basement CircuitsIf the breaker panel can be reached while you stand somewhere dry, switch off the circuits for the basement. Call the utility when the panel itself sits in the flooded area.
  3. Look at the Pump From a DistanceLook to see whether the pump came unplugged, its outlet tripped or the float is pinned against the pit wall. Keep your hands out of the water.
  4. Assume the Water Is DirtyChildren and pets stay on the upper floor, anyone who has to enter wears rubber boots and gloves, and hands get washed after contact with anything wet.
  5. Save What Is Dry, Then CallTake pictures of the pump and the water level, bring valuables that are still dry upstairs, and phone us. Waiting for it to recede without help is a mistake.
Coverage

Does Insurance Cover It?

Sump pump losses are widely misunderstood when it comes to insurance. A standard homeowners policy usually excludes water that backs up or overflows from a sump, and flood insurance is a separate policy with its own rules. Unless you bought an endorsement for sump overflow or water backup there is often no coverage, and such endorsements are capped. Your declarations page will list it, or your agent can tell you. Our records of the failed pump, how high the water rose and what it damaged go into your claim file.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Pump Out the Basement

We bring our own pumps and extractors, so nothing depends on your failed sump. Standing water and silt are removed from the floor, the pit area and any low spots.

2

Remove Contaminated Materials

Carpet, padding, insulation and wet drywall that the water contacted are pulled by a crew in protective gear, bagged and removed from the property so the concrete and studs are open.

3

Wash and Disinfect

The slab, wall framing and remaining contents are washed down and treated with an antimicrobial product. Doing it ahead of drying keeps the air movers from spreading contaminants through the basement.

4

Dry and Verify

Dehumidifiers and air movers run until moisture meter readings on the framing and concrete return to normal, typically 3 to 5 days. Before we pull our equipment, the pit should have a pump that works.

Common Causes

What Causes It

  • Motor failure in a pump that has simply reached the end of its life.
  • Discharge piping that has come apart, been flattened or plugged up outdoors.
  • Gravel, silt or debris in the pit jamming the impeller or clogging the intake.
  • The pump being unplugged by accident or sitting on an outlet that tripped.
  • Check valve trouble, which makes the pump move the same water again and again.
Why Speed Matters

What the Water Damages

Finished Basement Walls

The wet zone inside a wall reaches well past the water mark, as drywall and insulation pull moisture up off the floor. Since it is contaminated water, we remove the wet bottom portion instead of drying it.

Wall Framing

Bottom plates rest directly on concrete, so they are the first framing to get wet. We clean, disinfect and dry the studs and plates after removing the wet drywall.

Basement Floor Coverings

Carpet and pad soaked by groundwater cannot be cleaned to a safe standard and are removed. Vinyl plank and laminate floors generally have to be pulled as well, since dirty water stays under them.

Boxes and Stored Items

Cardboard boxes, furniture legs, books and fabrics soak up contaminated water quickly. Anything porous that sat in it is hard to disinfect and will grow mold within days.
