Kitchen Water Damage Help in West New York

A kitchen has more water connections than any other room: the faucet, the drain, the disposal, the dishwasher and the refrigerator line. Nearly all of them are hidden inside a cabinet or behind an appliance. If one starts leaking, water heads for the back of the base cabinet and works its way under the floor. We help West New York homeowners find it, dry it, and save as much of the kitchen as possible.

Cabinets are the expensive part of this problem. Plywood and solid wood will often dry successfully, whereas a particleboard box puffs up quickly and never returns to size. Toe kicks come off and a few small, concealed holes are drilled so dry air can travel behind and below the cabinets, which lets cabinets and countertops stay put in many kitchens during drying.

Warning Signs

  • Warping, staining or a soft spot in the floor of the cabinet below the sink
  • Cabinet doors that stick or no longer line up with each other
  • A water bill that climbs, or a faint hiss or drip sound, with every tap turned off
  • Stale, damp air that hits you when the doors under the sink are opened
  • The bottom edges of cabinet side panels swelling or flaking apart
  • Along the cabinet line or beside the fridge, vinyl that feels soft or looks stained

Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

The Ice Maker Line

A few drops per minute can escape from the narrow tube that supplies the ice maker. Behind a fridge that never moves, that is enough to rot flooring and the wall behind.

Wet Wall Behind Cabinets

Supply and drain lines run through the wall behind the sink and dishwasher. When they leak, framing and drywall get wet behind the cabinets, and mold spreads where no one can see it.

Drips at the Sink Drain

Because it leaks just when the sink drains, a loose fitting or a worn disposal seal often goes unnoticed. The water carries food waste, which is why a wet sink cabinet smells and molds so fast.

Flooring Over Flooring

It is common for a kitchen floor to be laid right over the previous one. Trapped water sits between the two, with no way to evaporate and no sign on the surface.

Why Speed Matters

The Damage Timeline

First hour

Water Runs Under

A failed fitting or hose sends water under appliances and to the rear of the cabinets. Through edges and seams, it works its way under the floor covering.

Hours 24 to 48

Cabinets Start Swelling

The water is absorbed by particleboard bases and toe kicks, which swell. Mold can begin in the dark, closed space under the cabinets and behind the dishwasher.

Day 3 to 7

Flooring Starts to Move

Hardwood cups, laminate edges puff up, and vinyl starts to lift as its adhesive lets go. The doors and drawers on the cabinets go out of alignment.

After a week

Removal Becomes Likely

A cabinet box that has swollen is not coming back. Mold at the back of the cabinets often forces their removal, countertop included, and the subfloor may be delaminating.

Our Process

How We Handle Kitchen Water Damage

1

Stop the Source

First we close the line to the leaking appliance or fixture, slide out the dishwasher or fridge when needed, and identify the exact failed connection so the repair can happen ahead of drying.

2

Map the Moisture

We check the cabinet bases, the wall at their back and the floor throughout the room with thermal imaging and moisture meters. It is common for water beneath a kitchen floor to spread further than you would guess.

3

Dry Without Demolition

Toe kicks come off and air is directed under and behind the cabinets. Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, and we treat damp cavities with an antimicrobial.

4

Take Out Only What Failed

We carefully remove any flooring, subfloor or cabinet section that cannot be saved. Once readings confirm everything is dry, we put the appliances back and help you schedule whatever repairs are left.
