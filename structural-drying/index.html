Of all the stages in restoring water damage, drying the structure counts most, and it is the stage most often botched by people without training. Once standing water has been extracted, a great deal of moisture is still held inside the structure: in insulation, concrete, subfloors, wood framing, drywall and other components. Controlled evaporation and dehumidification, applied scientifically, are needed to get that moisture out before a wet structure develops mold, rotting wood, degraded materials and odors.

The structural drying technicians at West New York Restoration are IICRC-certified and trained in psychrometry, the science of how temperature, humidity and moisture in air relate. For each job they apply it to calculate airflow patterns, temperature settings and the placement of equipment that will give the best conditions for evaporation. Plaster, concrete, hardwood and drywall each dry at their own rate, so each needs its own approach to get down to an acceptable moisture level with no added damage.

Every day of the drying process, we check moisture using thermal imaging cameras, thermo-hygrometers and calibrated moisture meters. The result is a detailed log of readings that tracks how moisture comes down over time in each material that got wet. Those readings guide daily equipment adjustments that keep the drying as efficient as it can be. We call a job dry when every affected material meets the moisture standard in the IICRC S500 guidelines, and never just because a surface feels dry.

Signs You Need Structural Drying in West New York

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Blistered paint and lifting wallpaper on a wall that got wet
  • No visible water left, yet walls or floors still feel cold and damp
  • Drywall that is soft, swollen or shows moisture staining
  • Musty odors that continue after extraction, which suggests trapped moisture
  • Rooms that stay humid even with windows open and fans running
  • Moisture meter readings above normal in walls, floors or ceilings after water damage
  • Hardwood floors that are cupping, crowning or showing gaps between boards

Why Professional Structural Drying Matters

Structural drying is a science, not a guessing game. Secondary mold damage most often traces back to someone who put down fans and hoped things would dry. It involves daily monitoring, a calculation of the required grain depression (the capacity to remove moisture) and a working knowledge of the way materials absorb moisture and let it go. Get it wrong in one direction and mold feeds on leftover moisture, get it wrong in the other and wood or similar materials are damaged by over-drying.

The equipment used for professional structural drying is fundamentally different from consumer products. High-velocity air movers produce precise airflow patterns that speed up evaporation from the structure, and low-grain refrigerant (LGR) commercial dehumidifiers capture 10 to 20 times the moisture a home unit can. Machines like these cost a great deal and take training to use well, which explains why professional results are so much better than a do-it-yourself effort.

Structural Drying Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Plan for daily access so readings can be taken and equipment adjusted
  • If a new damp spot or smell shows up while drying is underway, let the technician know at once
  • The dehumidifiers and air movers should run around the clock until our technician takes them out
  • Trust the moisture meter readings, because surfaces can feel dry while moisture remains deep in materials
  • Do not place personal items on top of drying equipment or block the airflow from air movers
  • Deal with water intrusion right away, since an early start on drying gives a better result
