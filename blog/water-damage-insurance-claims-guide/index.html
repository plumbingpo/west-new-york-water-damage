Filing an insurance claim for water damage can be overwhelming, especially when you are already dealing with a flooded or damaged property. What Policies Usually Cover

In general, a standard homeowner's policy pays for water damage when the event was accidental and happened suddenly. This includes burst pipes, appliance failures (washing machines, water heaters, dishwashers), ice dam damage, accidental overflow from sinks or bathtubs, and sudden HVAC system failures. Coverage typically extends to water extraction, structural drying, material replacement, and related restoration costs, including temporary living expenses if your home is uninhabitable.

What's Typically NOT Covered

Standard policies usually exclude gradual damage from slow leaks or deferred maintenance, flooding from outside water sources (which requires separate flood insurance), sewage backup (which requires a specific endorsement), foundation seepage from groundwater, and damage from neglected maintenance. The time to review exclusions is before anything happens, while you can still buy extra policies or endorsements to cover what is missing.

Steps to File a Successful Claim

To keep your claim protected, begin by documenting the damage thoroughly, with pictures and video, while nothing has been cleaned up yet. Contact your insurance company promptly to report the loss. Mitigation matters too, meaning sensible action to stop further damage. Do not throw away anything that was damaged before the adjuster sees it. Have the work done by professionals who are IICRC-certified. Living expenses, temporary repairs, and any other cost connected to the loss should each have a saved receipt. Be present during the adjuster's inspection and point out all damage.
